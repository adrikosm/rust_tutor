# E04 design directions

Two local design-skill searches were used as provocations, then filtered through the product’s evidence-first subject matter and accessibility requirements.

## Direction A — Ownership ledger (implemented candidate)

A warm paper surface, editorial serif headlines, monospaced evidence labels, thin ledger rules, and a restrained Rust-orange lifetime rail make ownership transfer and proof feel native to the subject. The layout is content-first, high contrast, keyboard-first, light/dark capable, and uses system fonts so the offline app makes no font request.

Avoid: childlike education typography, gamified streaks, generic rounded-card grids, decorative animation, low-contrast beige, and color-only status.

## Direction B — Compiler blueprint

A deep slate workspace, technical sans/mono typography, blueprint grid, and cool blue focus indicators frame the product as a precise local developer tool. It suits focused practice and graph inspection, but requires more care to avoid neon “cyberpunk terminal” clichés and dark-mode fatigue.

Avoid: scanlines, glow, glitch motion, fake terminal chrome, dense HUD decoration, and a dark-only experience.

## Approved decision

Direction A is approved because it distinguishes reading/reasoning from a generic IDE while keeping code panels visually technical. Direction B remains a documented alternative, not a hidden theme. The owner authorized approval-gated completion on 2026-07-18; the implemented tokens, five-view walkthrough, focus treatment, reduced-motion behavior, responsive layouts, and light/dark contrast checks satisfy the E04 gates.
